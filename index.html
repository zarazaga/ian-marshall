About the artist

Ian Marshall

Ian Harley Marshall (1933–2016) was an architect, artist and maritime historian whose life and work crossed several continents. Born into a Scottish family, he spent part of the war years in Edinburgh, where visits to the Firth of Forth and Rosyth naval base with his older brother helped establish his lifelong fascination with ships. After the war he travelled by flying boat to join his parents in Cape Town and later studied architecture at the University of Cape Town.

Marshall practiced architecture in Britain, the United States and Africa. He studied architecture at the University of Pennsylvania under Louis Kahn before returning with his wife, Jean, to East Africa and Scotland. His architectural career eventually took the family to Botswana, where he designed hospitals, schools, university buildings and major government buildings, with a particular interest in climate-responsive and ecological design.

During the 1980s, Marshall increasingly returned to watercolor and the maritime subjects that had interested him since childhood. After moving to Maine, he devoted himself full-time to maritime painting, combining an architect’s understanding of structure with extensive historical research. He produced more than 500 maritime paintings and wrote and illustrated a series of books on ships and flying boats. His final major project was Victory at Sea, a collaboration with historian Paul Kennedy that brought together Marshall’s 53 paintings with Kennedy’s text to tell the story of naval power and the transformation of the global order during the Second World War.

A Fellow and past President of the American Society of Marine Artists and a member of Britain’s Royal Society of Marine Artists, Marshall exhibited internationally, including at Mystic Seaport, the U.S. Navy Museum and U.S. Naval Academy Museum. His work is held in public collections including the U.S. Naval Academy Museum, U.S. Naval War College, Royal Naval Museum, Maine Maritime Museum, National Museum of Botswana, Netherlands Naval Museum and Foynes Flying Boat Museum, as well as Lloyd’s of London.
